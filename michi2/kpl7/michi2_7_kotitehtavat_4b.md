Yes, it is understandable. I would make one small adjustment:

**寒いとき、洋服の上に着るものは何ですか？**
*Samui toki, yōfuku no ue ni kiru mono wa nan desu ka?*

→ **What do you wear over your clothes when it is cold?**

For a beginner-level question, this is perfectly good. You could also say **上着（うわぎ）**, meaning “outerwear/jacket”:

**寒いとき、洋服の上に着るものは何ですか？ → 上着です。**
“When it’s cold, what do you wear over your clothes?” → “Outerwear / a jacket.”

The Japanese term for jeans is **ジーンズ** (*jīnzu*). Alternatively, **ジーパン** (*jīpan* — short for "jeans pants") or **デニム** (*denimu* — denim) are also widely used.

Here are the fixes for your question and a natural response:

### Question Revision

* **Original:** わかものがよく履くズボンは日本語でなnといいですか
* **Corrected:** 若（わか）者（もの）がよく履（は）くズボンは日本語（にほんご）で**何（なん）と言（い）いますか**。
*(What do you call the pants that young people often wear in Japanese?)*

---

### Improved Answer Options

* **ジーンズ** (*jīnzu*): Standard word for jeans.
* **ジーパン** (*jīpan*): A very common casual abbreviation (Jeans + Pants).
* **デニム** (*denimu*): Frequently used when talking about trendier jeans or denim material.

**Complete Answer Sentence:**

> **ジーンズと言（い）います。** *(They are called jeans.)*
> *(or **ジーパンと言います**)*

The term you are looking for is **スリッパ** (*surippa* - slippers) or **ルームシューズ** (*ruumu shuuzu* - room shoes).

* **スリッパ** (*surippa*): The standard, most common term for indoor slippers.
